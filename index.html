How to use Better Video Analysis

  1. Load a video of something moving across the frame (a rolling ball, a walking person, a car). It stays on your device — nothing is uploaded.
  2. Set the frame rate (fps) to match your video so times are correct. Most phone video is 30 fps (slow-motion is often 120 or 240).
  3. Calibrate the scale: click Set scale, then click two points a known real distance apart (a metre stick, a floor tile) and type that distance in metres. Now pixels become metres. Optionally Set origin to choose where 0 m is.
  4. Track the object:
    • ✋ Manual tap — step through frames and tap the object each time. Best on a touchscreen and always reliable.
    • 🤖 Auto (OpenCV) — click the object once to lock onto it, then Track to end and OpenCV follows it frame-to-frame.
  5. Add more objects to compare several movers, each its own color. Read the distance–time graph, add a best-fit line, and export CSV.
  6. Select & analyze: tap a point on the graph, table, or (in ✏️ Edit mode) the video — it highlights in all three, and the graph shows its coordinates and slope (a tangent line). Drag across the graph to select a range: the panel shows the range’s best-fit slope and integral, and the best-fit line is limited to that range. Delete selected removes bad points.

Tip: a straight distance–time line means constant speed (slope = speed); a curve means acceleration.
